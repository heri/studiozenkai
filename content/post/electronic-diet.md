Towards the end of ["About Heri"](https://studiozenkai.com/post/about-heri/), I wrote about the importance of regular exercise.

My personal definition of exercise is broader than most. It is doing something challenging for a short amount of time, where you need to use your physical and mental strength to go through and avoid terminating early.

First is physical exercise. You can either do cardio, which is sustaining lighter physical activity for long periods of time, or inversely intensity training. I do of mix of trail running, martial arts, weights, cold showers, plus the occasional hike and [serious handyman work](https://studiozenkai.com/post/lessons-from-vacation-property-airbnb/). Physical exercise not only make you look good, it brings a range of proven health and mental benefits, too many to list here. Lack of physical exercise is on the other hand a guarantee for early physical ailments and shorter life expectancy.

Second form of exercise is diet. It can be eating less, only eating within a time window, not eating certain foods, or not eating at all. A few years ago, I did not eat any type of artificial sugar or processed foods. It was a real challenge ! I have friends who stopped drinking alcohol for six months. On a regular basis (every 3 months), I do not eat for a whole weekend, ie for 48 hours straight. Why? Because in many ways, modern life encourages us towards over-eating, and also pushes us towards ultra processed foods. Severy cutting your diet is a way to discover your dependency towards this system, and also to affirm that one or another, you control what you eat. There are also multiple proven health benefits.

Third form of exercise is what I call "financial diet". Once a year, I pledge not to spend anything for a month, except essentials such as basic food and taxes. Forget Amazon purchases, streaming subscriptions, trips to the coffee shop, ski trips or getting a treat for the kids. This can be very hard for social settings. How do you tell friends you cannot have lunch because you pledge not to spend anything? What if a family member needs something? :sweat: Like running a marathon, it is hard and you are tempted to dump the "stupid" challenge and grab instead an ice cream. But it is liberating to see that many expenses that were previously seen as "obligations" were just obligations because of habits and assumptions.

I am about to start a fourth form of exercise that I call electronic diet. I pledge not to use any electronic device, except for pure work purposes. This means I won't be able to pay bills electronically, nor will I be able to watch movies or TV series, and of course Internet won't be possible in daily life. I am flooking forward to do more physical activities, read more books, and play more card and board games with kids. The idea here is to break free from the array of electronic devices controlling our physical and mental life. Like the food or financial diet, it is not a statement that you cannot live without money or without electronic devices. Rather, it is a statement that it is always our choice to use electronic devices, and if we want, we could live without. 

See you in a month!
