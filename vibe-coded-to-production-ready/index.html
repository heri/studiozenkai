Why vibe-coded apps fail in production

Security

Anyone can probe it

Organizations with full IT departments still get breached. In August 2026, attackers stole the personal information of 350,000 people from Quebec's construction commission (La Presse). An app built over a few weekends has no security team at all.

Scale

It works on a laptop

It's fast with fifty test rows. Will it hold up with your whole database, a year of history, and your entire team logging in on Monday morning? Slow pages and timeouts usually show up the same week everyone starts relying on the app.

Upkeep

Who answers the tickets?

The person who built it had time that weekend. Will they have time in two weeks, when their real job is busy, leadership wants a change, and customer tickets start piling up?

Security, scale and upkeep. That's the part I take off your plate.

Frequently asked questions

Is vibe coding safe for production?

It can be, for the right app. Internal tools and early products built with AI can run safely in production once the basics are in place: proper sign-in and permissions, secrets kept out of the code, backups, monitoring and tests. The risk comes from skipping that layer, which AI tools rarely add on their own. The checklist above covers it.

How much does a vibe code audit cost?

$2,000 USD for a side project or internal tool, with a report in 2 business days. $4,000 USD for a critical application that handles sensitive customer data or needs to scale, with a report in 4 business days. Both are fixed price, and the fee is credited toward hardening if you go ahead.

Will you tell me to throw it away and start over?

Rarely. In my experience, most AI-built apps have a sound core and a missing layer around it. I'll only recommend a rebuild if fixing would cost more, and I'll show you the numbers.

I'm not technical. Will I understand the report?

Yes. It's written for the person who owns the risk, not for engineers. Each finding says what could happen, how likely it is, and what fixing it costs.

Do you work with apps built in Lovable, Replit, Bolt, Cursor or Claude Code?

Yes. I work on apps built with Lovable, Bolt, Replit, v0, Cursor, Claude Code and GitHub Copilot, in Python, TypeScript, Elixir and Ruby, with the usual databases and cloud hosts. If I'm not the right fit, I'll say so on the first call.

You're one person. What happens when you're away?

You work directly with me: no account managers, and no juniors learning on your app. I work Monday to Friday, Eastern time, and I tell you about time off weeks in advance. I won't pick up the phone on December 25, so part of my job is making sure you never need me to: alerts, a written runbook, a tested rollback, and documentation good enough that another engineer could take over tomorrow. That's the last item on the checklist, and I hold my own work to it.
